Stellar Outbreak

Stellar Outbreak is a sci-fi action game created with friends from Strong Boat Studio and developed with support from Woodland Games.

In the game, you play as a scientist on a space mission, sent deep into the unknown to conduct a crucial experiment for your country. However, the mission quickly spirals out of control when a mysterious virus is released onboard. Humans begin to mutate, and it becomes clear that returning to Earth was never part of the plan.

The most interesting part of this project was the enemy AI system. It was my first fully functional AI system for enemies, and I still consider it one of the most enjoyable systems I`ve worked on.

The enemies behave dynamically. They can patrol, chase the player, attack, die, and can also interact with environmental objects such as trash.

The player is able to hide from enemies inside lockers, which adds a stealth layer to the gameplay and increases tension.

Development Notes ▾

Project Challenges

We made Stellar Outbreak under the patronage of Woodland Games. For a lot of us, it was the first time working in a real production environment with a set pipeline, deadlines, meetings, and regular progress updates.

  • Designing the AI system at a time when tools like ChatGPT were not available, which required pored over documentation, tried things out, and reworked code again and again.
  • The logic used was too complex based on “if” conditions, which made the system difficult to maintain and expand.
  • Frequent merge conflicts and scene management issues during early production stages.
  • Designing a dialogue system that can handle all game scenarios while maintaining a consistent narrative flow.

How We Solved It

We adopted an iterative approach, refactoring existing systems. Each solution was implemented progressively as issues appeared, allowing us to continuously improve code quality and team coordination.

  • We started small with AI, layering in simple behaviors and building them up through testing and debugging.
  • We refactored the AI into a state machine architecture, separating behaviors into clear states such as patrol, chase, and attack.
  • We nailed down a prefab-based workflow and set some clearer rules for scenes and assets management. The merge conflicts pretty much vanished, and the team started moving together.
  • For NPCs, we linked their dialogue system directly to quest states, so conversations flowed naturally and changed according to what the player had done.

What I would do differently

Looking back, this project was one of the most important learning experiences in my development journey. The pressure to meet real deadlines and communicate well forced all of us to grow up fast as developers and solve complex problems as a team.

If I could change one thing, it would be finishing and releasing the game before the team eventually split and moved in different directions for university. Although the project never saw a full public release, the experience itself became incredibly valuable.
