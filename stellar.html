Stellar Outbreak

Stellar Outbreak is a sci-fi action game created in collaboration with friends from Strong Boat Studio and developed in cooperation with Woodland Games.

The player takes on the role of a scientist and crew member of a space expedition sent to conduct a high-level experiment for the sake of the nation. However, the mission quickly spirals out of control when a mysterious virus is released onboard. Humans begin to mutate, and it becomes clear that returning to Earth was never part of the plan.

The most interesting part of this project was the enemy AI system. From today’s perspective, it was my first fully functional AI system for enemies, and I still consider it one of the most enjoyable systems I’ve worked on.

The enemies behave dynamically — they patrol, chase the player, attack, die, and can also interact with environmental objects such as trash.

The player is able to hide from enemies inside lockers, which adds a stealth layer to the gameplay and increases tension during encounters.

Development Notes ▾

Project Challenges

Stellar Outbreak was a team project developed under the patronage of Woodland Games. The production followed a structured pipeline with regular milestones, meetings, and progress reports, which introduced us to a real production environment for the first time.

  • Designing the AI system at a time when tools like ChatGPT were not available, which required heavy reliance on documentation, experimentation, and repeated code iterations.
  • Early reliance on overly complex “if-based” logic, which made the system hard to maintain and extend.
  • Frequent merge conflicts and scene management issues during early production stages.
  • Designing a dialogue system capable of handling all in-game scenarios while maintaining consistent narrative flow.

How We Solved It

To address the challenges we encountered during production, we focused on iterative development, refactoring existing systems, and introducing more structured workflows. Each solution was implemented progressively as issues appeared, allowing us to continuously improve both code quality and team coordination throughout the project.

  • We approached AI development iteratively, starting with simple behaviors and gradually combining them into a more complex system through continuous testing and debugging.
  • We refactored the AI into a state machine architecture, separating behaviors into clear states such as patrol, chase, and attack, which improved readability and scalability.
  • We introduced a structured prefab-based workflow and clearer rules for scene and asset management, significantly reducing merge conflicts and improving team coordination.
  • We implemented a NPC system connected with the dialogue system, which dynamically switched dialogue lines based on the current quest state. This allowed conversations to adapt to player progress and ensured consistent narrative flow across different gameplay situations.

What I would do differently

Looking back, this project was one of the most important learning experiences in my development journey. Although we were working under real production constraints and strict milestones, it forced us to quickly adapt, communicate effectively, and solve complex problems as a team.

If I could change one thing, it would be finishing and releasing the game before the team eventually split and moved in different directions for university. Despite the project never reaching a full public release, the experience itself became extremely valuable.
