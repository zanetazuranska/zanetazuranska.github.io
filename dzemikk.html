Kahun - Custom 3D Engine (C++ / OpenGL)

Kahun is a hex-based action-adventure game inspired by Tiki culture, developed entirely on our custom C++ / OpenGL game engine.

Players step into the shoes of a young shaman using magical runes to fight enemys, explore island and try to rescue his kidnapped son from dark forces.

Development Notes ▾

Project Challenges

We pulled all this together in just four months. We had to build the engine, the editor, and the game all at once. The biggest challenge was keeping production organized and moving fast, all while constantly iterating on core systems.

  • Delivering a complete engine, editor, and game within 4 months required strict planning and a very disciplined workflow.
  • Motion capture integration was new for us and it brought a lot of complexity.
  • Performance and optimization challenges typical for custom engine development.
  • Designing enemy AI that supports strategic gameplay, allowing players to plan not just reacting randomly.

How We Solved It

The project succeeded thanks to strong scheduling discipline and iterative development.

  • We kept our goals tightly scoped and made sure everyone knew what to focus on for each phase.
  • To handle it, we split off a smaller sub-team just for mocap, so the rest of us could stay focused on the main engine and gameplay.
  • Asset system was designed to prevent duplication, while rendering uses instancing, frustum culling, and backface culling. Collision systems rely on octree, and many gameplay systems use dirty-flag optimization to avoid unnecessary updates.
  • AI was implemented as a hybrid system combining Utility-Based AI and Behaviour Trees, allowing enemies to select actions dynamically based on game state and player behavior.

What I Would Do Differently

Looking back, I would`ve put more energy into visuals and polish much earlier. We just ran out of time at the end for that extra level of presentation.

Still, Kahun was a huge learning experience. It brought together engine development, editor tools, and gameplay design. I came away with a much stronger sense of what a full-scale game pipeline looks like.
